What is ADAS?

Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) are now standard in modern vehicles and directly impact how repairs are performed. From sensor alignment to windshield calibration, understanding ADAS is essential for restoring safety systems after a collision.

Common ADAS Questions

Recalibration means resetting ADAS sensors after repairs. This is critical after replacing windshields, bumpers, or suspension components, or after any structural work that could affect sensor alignment.

When a windshield is replaced, the forward-facing camera mounted behind it must be recalibrated. This ensures systems like lane departure warning and adaptive cruise control function properly.

Calibration means ensuring all ADAS sensors are properly aligned and communicating with the vehicle’s control systems. It’s a key step in post-repair safety validation.

ADAS sensors may be found:

  • Behind the windshield (camera)
  • In the front and rear bumpers (radar and sonar)
  • In side mirrors (blind spot detection)
  • Around the vehicle perimeter (ultrasonic sensors)

Technicians must inspect these areas after any impact.

ADAS helps prevent future accidents. Proper calibration after repairs ensures the vehicle’s safety systems are fully functional, protecting both the driver and others on the road.

Technicians should be aware of these SAE levels:

  • Level 0: No automation
  • Level 1–2: Driver assistance (ADAS)
  • Level 3–5: Increasing levels of autonomy

Most collision repair work today involves Level 1–2 systems, which require technician intervention for calibration.

Dynamic calibration often involves self-calibration, where the vehicle adjusts its sensors during a test drive under specific conditions.

Car calibration ensures that all safety systems are functioning as intended. It’s a critical final step in the repair process, especially for vehicles with ADAS.

Safety devices like airbags, seatbelts, and ADAS systems must be inspected and tested after a collision. Proper repair and calibration ensure they’ll perform correctly in future incidents.
